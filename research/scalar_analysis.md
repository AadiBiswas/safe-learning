# Stage 0 — Independent analysis of the scalar model

*2026-11-20. Every numbered claim below is proved here unless it is labelled otherwise. Numerical checks are in `scripts/scalar_check.py`, with output in `results/scalar_check.txt`. They illustrate the claims and are not part of any proof.*

## 0. Summary

1. **Maximum safe intensity is worst-case optimal** for every objective that is monotone under inclusion of the consistent set. That covers the upper bound, the diameter, the certified-safe action set, and exact identification. It holds at every horizon and for every $p > 0$ (Prop. S2). In this model a deliberately less informative experiment **never** helps.
2. **The recurrence in the brief is correct** for a *fixed* $\theta^\*$, with the adversary $\eta_t \equiv +\delta$, which is always admissible (Prop. S3).
   - Under that adversary the lower endpoint is pinned: $L_t = \theta^\*$ for every $t \ge 1$.
   - So the "deadlock" of the upper bound coexists with *exact* knowledge of the lower endpoint. Learning stops at one end only.
3. **The critical value $\lambda_c(p) = (p-1)^{p-1}/p^p$ ($p > 1$) is correct**, and so is $8\delta\theta^\* = 1$ at $p = 2$, with the stated fixed points. The complete fixed-point and basin structure for every $p > 0$ is in Prop. S4.
4. **New, and it changes the picture.** If the worst case is taken *uniformly* over $\theta^\* \in \Theta_0$ (the usual meaning of a guarantee), there is no threshold at all. The irreducible worst-case diameter is exactly
   $$\min\bigl(U_0 - L_0,\ 2\delta U_0^{\,p}\bigr),$$
   it is reached after **one** experiment, and no later experiment improves it (Prop. S5). The $\lambda_c$ phenomenon is an *instance-dependent* statement: it describes the truths $\theta^\*$ that are far from the top of the prior.
5. **Total deadlock** (the worst-case consistent set never shrinks at all, for any policy) holds iff $2\delta U_0^{\,p} \ge U_0 - L_0$. This condition does not involve $\theta^\*$. For $p > 1$ and $L_0 \ge 0$ it holds whenever $U_0 \ge (2\delta)^{-1/(p-1)}$: a sufficiently conservative prior upper bound makes learning impossible for *every* truth (Cor. S6).
6. **Near criticality**, the time to pass the bottleneck scales as $(\lambda_c - \lambda)^{-1/2}$ (saddle-node). For $p = 2$ the constant is explicit (§6). There are discontinuities in the asymptotic uncertainty, as a function of $\delta$ or of the prior, at the unstable fixed point (§7).
7. **Worst-case versus favourable data.** With $\delta > 0$, worst-case exact identification never happens. Favourable disturbances identify $\theta^\*$ exactly in two experiments.

The brief's motivating claim ("two agents with identical sensors and policies may end differently solely because their priors differ") is **true** in the instance-dependent sense (§5). Under the uniform criterion it reduces to the trivial statement that the one-shot diameter depends on $U_0$.

## 1. Model and conventions

- Truth $\theta^\* > 0$, with prior interval $\Theta_0 = [L_0, U_0] \ni \theta^\*$, $U_0 > 0$.
- Action $x \ge 0$. It is safe iff $\theta^\* x \le 1$, where equality counts as safe.
- Observation $Y = \theta^\* x^p + \eta$, with $|\eta| \le \delta$ and $p > 0$.
- Consistent set update: $\Theta_{t+1} = \Theta_t \cap \{\theta : |Y_t - \theta x_t^p| \le \delta\}$. For $x_t > 0$ this is the interval $\Theta_t \cap [(Y_t-\delta)x_t^{-p},\ (Y_t+\delta)x_t^{-p}]$.
- Robust safety: $\theta x \le 1$ for all $\theta \in \Theta_t$. Since $x \ge 0$, this is $x \le 1/U_t$. The certified-safe action set is $\mathcal S_t = [0, 1/U_t]$.

**What "the adversary" means.** The disturbance sequence is chosen adversarially and adaptively, with full knowledge of the policy. Two worst cases are used and kept apart:
- **instance-dependent**: $\sup_\eta$ with $\theta^\*$ fixed;
- **uniform**: $\sup_{\theta^\* \in \Theta_0}\sup_\eta$.

Consistency is automatic: data generated by $\theta^\*$ with $|\eta| \le \delta$ always leave $\theta^\* \in \Theta_t$. This answers question 2 of the brief: **any** sequence with $|\eta_t| \le \delta$ is consistent, in particular $\eta_t \equiv \delta$.

**Model assumption worth flagging: $x \ge 0$.** If negative intensities were allowed (and $x^p$ were defined for them, e.g. $p = 1$), then $\theta x \le 1$ would hold for every $x \le 0$ whenever $\theta > 0$. Arbitrarily large $|x|$ would then give arbitrarily fine resolution, and identification would be immediate. The deadlock phenomena depend on the action set being one-sided relative to the risk. The same issue reappears in the linear multidimensional model (`research/research_directions.md`, Direction C).

## 2. One experiment

> **Lemma S1.** For $x > 0$, the set of possible new consistent sets is
> $$\{\Theta_t \cap I : I \text{ a closed interval of length } w(x) := 2\delta x^{-p} \text{ containing } \theta^\*\}.$$
> The adversary chooses the position of $I$ freely, subject to $\theta^\* \in I$.

*Proof.* $I = [(Y-\delta)x^{-p}, (Y+\delta)x^{-p}] = [\theta^\* + (\eta-\delta)x^{-p},\ \theta^\* + (\eta+\delta)x^{-p}]$. As $\eta$ ranges over $[-\delta, \delta]$, the left end ranges over $[\theta^\* - w, \theta^\*]$. ∎

Since $p > 0$, $w(x)$ is strictly decreasing in $x$, so the safe maximum $x = 1/U_t$ gives the smallest window, $w = 2\delta U_t^{\,p}$.

## 3. Optimality of maximum safe intensity

> **Proposition S2 (domination).** Let $J$ be any objective that is non-increasing under inclusion of the final consistent set. The adversary maximises $J$; the agent minimises it. Examples: $U_T$, $\operatorname{diam}\Theta_T$, $\mathbf 1[\Theta_T \text{ not a singleton}]$, $-|\mathcal S_T|$. Then for every horizon $T$, every $\theta^\*$ and every (possibly adaptive, randomised) policy $\pi$,
> $$\sup_{\eta}\, J(\Theta^\pi_T) \;\ge\; \sup_\eta\, J(\Theta^{\max}_T),$$
> where "max" plays $x_t = 1/U_t$. Taking $\sup_{\theta^\*}$ on both sides gives the uniform version.

*Proof (coupling).* Fix any adversary sequence $\eta^{\max}$ against the max policy. We build an adversary against $\pi$ with $\Theta^\pi_t \supseteq \Theta^{\max}_t$ for all $t$. Suppose this holds at $t$. Then:
- $U^\pi_t \ge U^{\max}_t$, so $\pi$'s action satisfies $x^\pi \le 1/U^\pi_t \le x^{\max}$, and $w^\pi \ge w^{\max}$. If $x^\pi = 0$, then $\Theta^\pi$ is unchanged and the step is trivial.
- Let $I^{\max} = [a, a + w^{\max}] \ni \theta^\*$. Choose $I^\pi = [b, b + w^\pi]$ with $b = a - (w^\pi - w^{\max})/2$.
  - Then $I^\pi \supseteq I^{\max} \ni \theta^\*$.
  - So by Lemma S1, $I^\pi$ is realised by an admissible $\eta^\pi$.
- Hence $\Theta^\pi_{t+1} = \Theta^\pi_t \cap I^\pi \supseteq \Theta^{\max}_t \cap I^{\max} = \Theta^{\max}_{t+1}$.

Randomised $\pi$ is handled pathwise. Monotonicity of $J$ finishes the proof. ∎

*Check.* §C of `results/scalar_check.txt`: 80,000 coupled steps with random policies and random adversaries, with 0 inclusion violations.

**Consequences.**
- **(Brief, question 1)** Maximum intensity is optimal for the worst-case upper bound, at every horizon.
- **(Question 10)** In the scalar model, the upper bound, the diameter, exact identification and expansion of the certified-safe set all share the *same* optimal policy. **No policy separation can exist here.** Any separation must come from richer structure: several directions, a non-monotone safety/information trade-off, or dynamics.
- **(Direction A)** "Can a deliberately less informative experiment produce a more favourable long-term uncertainty evolution?" **No**, in this model, under worst-case disturbances. Under *favourable* disturbances the question is moot (§8).

## 4. The upper-bound recurrence (fixed $\theta^\*$)

> **Proposition S3.**
> - **(a)** Under the max policy, for any adversary,
>   $$U_{t+1} = \min\bigl(U_t,\ \theta^\* + (\eta_t + \delta) U_t^{\,p}\bigr) \le \min\bigl(U_t,\ \theta^\* + 2\delta U_t^{\,p}\bigr) =: F(U_t).$$
>   Equality holds for $\eta_t = \delta$.
> - **(b)** Since $F$ is non-decreasing, the constant adversary $\eta \equiv \delta$ maximises $U_T$ for every $T$: $U_T \le F^T(U_0)$.
> - **(c)** Under $\eta \equiv \delta$, $L_t = \max(L_{t-1}, \theta^\*) = \theta^\*$ for every $t \ge 1$.

*Proof.* (a) is the right end of the Lemma S1 window. (b) follows by induction: $U_{t+1} \le F(U_t) \le F(F^t(U_0))$. (c): the left end is $(Y - \delta)U_t^{\,p} = \theta^\*$. ∎

**Dimensionless form.** With $v = U/\theta^\*$ and $\lambda = 2\delta(\theta^\*)^{p-1}$,
$$v_{t+1} = g(v_t) := \min(v_t,\ 1 + \lambda v_t^{\,p}).$$
There is strict progress at $v$ iff $\varphi(v) := v - 1 - \lambda v^p > 0$. Note $\varphi(1) = -\lambda \le 0$, so the map never pushes $U$ below $\theta^\*$, as it must not.

## 5. Fixed points, basins and thresholds

> **Proposition S4.** Let $\delta > 0$, so $\lambda > 0$. Write $\bar g(v) = 1 + \lambda v^p$, so that $g(v) = \min(v, \bar g(v))$.
> - **(i) $p = 1$.** $\varphi(v) = (1-\lambda)v - 1$.
>   - If $\lambda < 1$: the unique fixed point of $\bar g$ is $v^\* = 1/(1-\lambda)$. For $v_0 > v^\*$, $v_t \downarrow v^\*$ geometrically at rate $\lambda$, and $v_t - v^\* = \lambda^t(v_0 - v^\*)$. For $v_0 \le v^\*$ the upper bound is stuck.
>   - If $\lambda \ge 1$ (i.e. $\delta \ge 1/2$): no progress from any $v_0$.
>   - Here $\lambda = 2\delta$ does not involve $\theta^\*$.
> - **(ii) $0 < p < 1$.** $\varphi$ is strictly convex, $\varphi(1) < 0$ and $\varphi \to \infty$. So there is exactly one root $v^\* > 1$. For every $\lambda > 0$ and every $v_0 > v^\*$, $v_t \downarrow v^\*$, and $v_0 \le v^\*$ is stuck. There is no threshold in $\lambda$, and far priors always shrink.
> - **(iii) $p > 1$.** $\varphi$ is strictly concave, with maximiser $v_m = (\lambda p)^{-1/(p-1)}$ and $\varphi(v_m) = v_m(1 - 1/p) - 1$. Let $\lambda_c = (p-1)^{p-1}/p^p$.
>   - **$\lambda < \lambda_c$:** there are two roots $1 < v_- < v_+$. On $(v_-, v_+)$, $v_t \downarrow v_-$, with linear rate $\bar g'(v_-) = \lambda p v_-^{p-1} < 1$. Every $v_0 \le v_-$ or $v_0 \ge v_+$ is stuck: $g(v_0) = v_0$.
>   - **$\lambda = \lambda_c$:** there is a double root $v_c = p/(p-1)$. $\varphi \le 0$ everywhere, so no strict progress from anywhere.
>   - **$\lambda > \lambda_c$:** $\varphi < 0$ everywhere, so there is never progress.
> - **(iv) $p = 2$, in dimensional form.** $\lambda_c = 1/4 \iff 8\delta\theta^\* = 1$. The roots in the $U$-coordinate are $U_\pm = \bigl(1 \pm \sqrt{1 - 8\delta\theta^\*}\bigr)/(4\delta)$.

*Proof.*
- (i) and (ii) are direct.
- (iii): $\varphi'(v) = 1 - \lambda p v^{p-1}$ vanishes only at $v_m$. Then $\varphi(v_m) > 0 \iff v_m > p/(p-1) \iff \lambda < \lambda_c$, and $v_m > 1$ then.
- Convergence on $(v_-, v_+)$: $\bar g$ is increasing, so $\bar g(v) > \bar g(v_-) = v_-$ and $\bar g(v) < v$. Thus $v_t$ decreases and stays above $v_-$. Its limit is a fixed point of $\bar g$ in $[v_-, v_0)$, which is $v_-$.
- Rate: $\varphi'(v_-) > 0$ gives $\bar g'(v_-) < 1$.
- (iv) is the quadratic formula. ∎

*Checks* (`results/scalar_check.txt`):
- §A: $\lambda_c$ is reproduced to $10^{-16}$ for six values of $p$.
- §B: the $p = 2$ roots agree to $2 \times 10^{-15}$.
- §F, §G: the basins and the $p \le 1$ limits.

**Interpretation of (iii).**
- The **lower** fixed point $\theta^\* v_-$ is the asymptotic upper bound: there is irreducible uncertainty $\theta^\*(v_- - 1) > 0$ above the truth.
- The **upper** fixed point is a barrier. A prior with $U_0 \ge \theta^\* v_+$ never improves its upper bound, while a prior with $U_0 \in (\theta^\* v_-, \theta^\* v_+)$ does. So two agents with identical sensors and policies diverge solely through $U_0$. This confirms the brief's claim, **instance-wise**.

## 6. Behaviour at and near criticality ($p > 1$)

- **At $\lambda = \lambda_c$.** Nothing moves: $\varphi \le 0$ everywhere, so $U_t = U_0$ for all $t$ under $\eta \equiv \delta$, even from $v_0 = v_c$. There is no slow convergence; the iteration is just stationary.
- **Just below.** Start at $v_c = p/(p-1)$, which lies in $(v_-, v_+)$ whenever $\lambda < \lambda_c$, since $\varphi(v_c) > 0$ there.
  - Write $z = v - 2$, $\epsilon = \lambda_c - \lambda$. For $p = 2$ the map is $z' = z + z^2/4 - \epsilon(2+z)^2$. Near $z = 0$ this is the normal form of a saddle-node: $dz/dn \approx -(4\epsilon - z^2/4)$.
  - Integrating from $0$ to $s\,z_-$, where $z_- = -4\sqrt\epsilon$, gives
    $$n \approx \frac{\operatorname{artanh}(s)}{\sqrt{\lambda_c - \lambda}}.$$
  - *Check.* With $s = 0.99$: $\operatorname{artanh}(0.99) = 2.6467$, and §F of the checks gives $n\sqrt\epsilon \to 2.646$, with fitted exponent $-0.506$.
- **General $p > 1$.** The same normal-form argument gives $n = \Theta\bigl((\lambda_c - \lambda)^{-1/2}\bigr)$, with a $p$-dependent constant. This is not computed beyond $p = 2$.
- **Final approach.** The rate $\bar g'(v_-) = 1 - \Theta\bigl(\sqrt{\lambda_c - \lambda}\bigr)$, so the final approach to $v_-$ has the same scaling.

## 7. Dependence on the prior; discontinuities

Fix $v_0 > v_c$ and let $\lambda_1(v_0)$ be the value at which $v_+(\lambda_1) = v_0$.
- **At $\lambda = \lambda_1$:** $v_0$ is a fixed point, so $\lim v_t = v_0$.
- **For $\lambda$ slightly below $\lambda_1$:** $v_0 < v_+$, and $\lim v_t = v_-(\lambda) \to v_-(\lambda_1) < v_0$.

So the asymptotic upper bound **jumps** as $\delta$ decreases through the corresponding value. **Arbitrarily small improvements in precision can therefore change the asymptotic outcome discontinuously** (Direction A). The same jump occurs in $U_0$ at $U_0 = \theta^\* v_+$. Near the jump, the time to leave the neighbourhood of $v_+$ diverges logarithmically in $(v_+ - v_0)^{-1}$.

## 8. Uniform worst case: the one-shot trap

> **Proposition S5.** Let $\delta > 0$ and $p > 0$, and put $w_0 = 2\delta U_0^{\,p}$. Then
> $$\inf_\pi \sup_{\theta^\* \in \Theta_0} \sup_\eta \operatorname{diam}\Theta_T = \min(U_0 - L_0,\ w_0) \qquad \text{for every } T \ge 1,$$
> and also for the limit $T \to \infty$. The maximum-intensity policy attains it.

*Proof.*
- **Upper bound.** At $t = 0$ the max policy produces a window of length $w_0$, so $\operatorname{diam}\Theta_1 \le \min(U_0 - L_0, w_0)$. Consistent sets only shrink.
- **Lower bound.** Let $K = [\max(L_0, U_0 - w_0),\ U_0]$ and take $\theta^\* \in K$.
  - Every safe action satisfies $x \le 1/U_t$. As long as $\Theta_t \supseteq K$, $U_t = U_0$.
  - The windows of all $\theta \in K$ have a common point: $\bigcap_{\theta \in K}[\theta x^p - \delta,\ \theta x^p + \delta] \ne \varnothing \iff \operatorname{diam}(K)\,x^p \le 2\delta$. This holds since $x^p \le U_0^{-p}$.
  - The adversary reports such a common $y$. That is admissible, because $\theta^\* \in K$. Then $K \subseteq \Theta_{t+1}$, by induction for every $t$.
- By Prop. S2, no other policy does better. ∎

*Check.* §E: for three choices of $(p, \delta, U_0, L_0)$, the set stays exactly $[U_0 - w_0, U_0]$ for 50 steps.

**Reading.** In the uniform sense, the scalar game has **no** threshold and **no** dynamics. One experiment is all that can ever be guaranteed. The instance-dependent picture of §§4–7 describes the truths that lie *below* the trap: $\theta^\* < U_0 - w_0$, for which the adversary cannot keep $U$ at $U_0$.

$K$ is exactly a *self-preserving indistinguishable set* in the sense of the brief's Direction B. The scalar model is therefore a first instance of the trap characterisation of `research/finite_analysis.md`. In the instance-dependent case, the limit set $[\theta^\*, \theta^\* v_-]$ is also such a set, **with equality**:
$$2\delta(\theta^\* v_-)^p = \theta^\*\lambda v_-^p = \theta^\*(v_- - 1).$$
So the asymptotic uncertainty set lies exactly on the boundary of the trap condition.

> **Corollary S6 (total deadlock).** The worst-case consistent set never shrinks, for any policy and every $\theta^\* \in \Theta_0$, iff $2\delta U_0^{\,p} \ge U_0 - L_0$. For $p > 1$ and $L_0 \ge 0$, this holds whenever $U_0 \ge U_{\rm dead} := (2\delta)^{-1/(p-1)}$.

*Proof.*
- "If": with $K = \Theta_0$, the common-observation argument of S5 applies at every step.
- "Only if": if $w_0 < U_0 - L_0$, the first max-intensity experiment shrinks the set, whatever $\eta$ is.
- For $p > 1$: $U_0 \ge U_{\rm dead} \iff 2\delta U_0^{\,p-1} \ge 1 \iff w_0 \ge U_0 \ge U_0 - L_0$. ∎

*Other cases.*
- For $p = 1$, total deadlock needs $\delta \ge 1/2$ or $U_0(1 - 2\delta) \le L_0$.
- For $p < 1$, large priors are never totally deadlocked, since $U_0^p = o(U_0)$.

The exponent $p$ thus decides whether *more prior uncertainty makes learning harder in the strong sense*. When $p > 1$, the resolution at the largest safe experiment, $2\delta U^p$, grows faster than the uncertainty itself.

## 9. Exact identification, and favourable versus worst-case data

- **$\delta = 0$.** One experiment at any $x > 0$ gives $\theta^\* = Y/x^p$.
- **$\delta > 0$, worst case.** The diameter tends to the limits above, which are positive. In particular, instance-wise $\theta^\*(v_\infty - 1) > 0$, where $v_\infty$ is $v_-$ or $v_0$.
- **$\delta > 0$, favourable data.** $\eta = +\delta$ followed by $\eta = -\delta$ gives $L = \theta^\*$ and then $U = \theta^\*$ (§H of the checks). So "deadlock" is a property of worst-case sequences, not of the information structure. **The brief's warning is borne out.** Under the worst case for the upper bound, the agent still learns the lower endpoint exactly. What is lost is the guarantee, not all information.
- **Finite-time improvement versus identification (question 9).**
  - In the instance-wise basin $(v_-, v_+)$, the upper bound improves at every step, and never reaches $\theta^\* v_-$ in finite time.
  - It reaches any level above $\theta^\* v_-$ in finite time.
  - The uniform guarantee improves only once, at $t = 1$.

## 11. What was wrong or incomplete in the preliminary derivation

- **Nothing in the algebra.** The recurrence, $\lambda_c$, the $p = 2$ condition and the fixed points are all correct.
- **Incomplete:** the derivation tracked only the upper endpoint. Under its own worst case, the lower endpoint is identified exactly at $t = 1$.
- **Easy to misread:** the $\lambda_c$ threshold is instance-dependent, because $\lambda$ involves the unknown $\theta^\*$. The uniform worst case has no threshold and a one-step answer (S5). Any statement in the paper of the form "learning is possible iff $8\delta\theta^\* < 1$" must say that it is per-instance, and that the agent cannot evaluate it, since it does not know $\theta^\*$.
- **Unstated assumption:** $x \ge 0$, discussed in §1.
