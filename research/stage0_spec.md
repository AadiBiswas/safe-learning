# Can You Learn Without Making Mistakes? — Stage 0 Specification

*2026-11-20. Rev 0.*

**Status legend:**
- **[Def]** definition;
- **[Proved]** proved in a cited file;
- **[Conj]** conjecture;
- **[Num]** numerical observation;
- **[Open]** question.

## 1. Primitives

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $\Theta_0 \subseteq \mathbb R^d$ | prior parameter set; the truth satisfies $\theta^\* \in \Theta_0$ |
| $\mathcal U$ | action (experiment) set |
| $g(\theta, u)$ | safety function; $u$ is physically safe for $\theta$ iff $g(\theta, u) \le 0$ |
| $h(\theta, u)$, $\mathcal W$ | observation map and disturbance set; $Y = h(\theta^\*, u) + \eta$, $\eta \in \mathcal W$ |
| $\Theta_t$ | consistent (feasible) parameter set after $t$ experiments |
| $\varepsilon \ge 0$ | identification tolerance |
| $T \in \mathbb N \cup \{\infty\}$ | horizon |

**Model scope for Stage 0.**
- Safety is deterministic and hard.
- Disturbances are deterministic, bounded and adversarial ("unknown but bounded"; set-membership).
- There are no dynamics: Directions A–C. Dynamics enter only in Direction D.

Probabilistic safety and stochastic noise are separate later models (§6). They must not be conflated with this one.

## 2. Information state and admissible policies

- **[Def] Consistent set.** $\Theta_{t+1} = \{\theta \in \Theta_t : Y_t - h(\theta, u_t) \in \mathcal W\}$. It is non-increasing in $t$. It always contains $\theta^\*$, because the data are generated by $\theta^\*$.
- **[Def] Robust-safe action set.** $\mathcal S(K) = \bigcap_{\theta \in K}\{u : g(\theta, u) \le 0\}$. It is non-increasing in $K$: more uncertainty means fewer certified actions.
- **[Def] Admissible policy.** A map $\pi$ from histories $(u_0, Y_0, \dots, u_{t-1}, Y_{t-1})$ to actions with $\pi(\cdot) \in \mathcal S(\Theta_t)$ at every $t$. Equivalently, it is a function of $\Theta_t$ together with the history.
  - Randomisation is allowed, but it is irrelevant for the worst-case criteria below, by a pathwise argument.
  - A policy is **robustly safe** iff it is admissible. Admissibility guarantees $g(\theta^\*, u_t) \le 0$ for every $\theta^\* \in \Theta_0$ and every disturbance sequence. Conversely, an action outside $\mathcal S(\Theta_t)$ is unsafe for some $\theta$ still consistent with the data, and the adversary may choose that $\theta$ to be the truth.
- **[Def] Certified-safe region at time $t$:** $\mathcal S(\Theta_t)$. **Progressive expansion** means $\mathcal S(\Theta_t)$ strictly increases for infinitely many $t$, or reaches a target set.

## 3. Learning objectives (kept separate)

Each objective is stated worst-case. Two quantifier orders are always distinguished.
- **Uniform:** $\exists\pi\ \forall \theta^\* \in \Theta_0\ \forall \eta$.
- **Instance-wise:** for the given $\theta^\*$, $\forall \eta$. Either $\pi$ is fixed in advance, or $\pi$ may depend on $\theta^\*$; the second version is meaningless as a learning guarantee and is used only as a benchmark.

| Objective | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Exact identification** | $\Theta_t = \{\theta^\*\}$ at some finite $t$ |
| **$\varepsilon$-identification** | $\operatorname{diam}\Theta_t \le \varepsilon$ at some finite $t$, or in the limit $t \to \infty$ (asymptotic $\varepsilon$-identification). These two differ (see `research/scalar_analysis.md` §§5, 8). |
| **Upper-bound / one-sided learning** | improvement of $\sup\Theta_t$, or of a support function $h_{\Theta_t}(v)$, in a fixed direction |
| **Safe-region expansion** | growth of $\mathcal S(\Theta_t)$ |
| **Worst-case learning efficiency** | the minimax number of experiments to reach a target, $N^\*(\varepsilon) = \inf_\pi\sup_{\theta^\*,\eta}\inf\{t : \operatorname{diam}\Theta_t \le \varepsilon\}$ |

These objectives are **not** equivalent in general:
- reducing the diameter need not enlarge $\mathcal S$, and vice versa;
- identifying one endpoint is not identifying the parameter (scalar model: $L_t = \theta^\*$ while $U_t$ is stuck).

In the scalar model they happen to share an optimal policy (Prop. S2). Whether they separate in higher dimensions is open (Direction C).

**Favourable versus worst-case data.** "Learnable" always means learnable against worst-case disturbances unless stated otherwise. The *favourable* (best-case) analogue is recorded separately. In the scalar model, favourable data identify exactly in two steps.

## 4. Distinguishability, traps, deadlock

- **[Def] Safe distinguishability of $\theta, \theta'$ given $K \ni \theta, \theta'$.** There is $u \in \mathcal S(K)$ with $(h(\theta, u) + \mathcal W) \cap (h(\theta', u) + \mathcal W) = \varnothing$. *Pairwise* safe distinguishability, with $K = \{\theta, \theta'\}$, is strictly weaker than identifiability of a larger set (`research/finite_analysis.md`, non-locality example).
- **[Def] Trap (self-preserving indistinguishable set).** $K$ is nonempty and, for every $u \in \mathcal S(K)$, $\bigcap_{\theta \in K}(h(\theta, u) + \mathcal W) \ne \varnothing$.
- **[Def] Informational deadlock** at $\Theta_t$: $\Theta_t$ contains a trap of diameter $> \varepsilon$. **Total deadlock**: $\Theta_t$ itself is a trap.
- **[Def] Irreducible uncertainty.** $\inf_\pi\sup_{\theta^\*,\eta}\lim_{t}\operatorname{diam}\Theta_t$, together with its instance-wise analogue.
- **[Def] Safe identifiability.** Uniform $\varepsilon$-identifiability at $\varepsilon = 0$; *approximate* means $\varepsilon > 0$.
- **[Proved]** In finite spaces, uniform $\varepsilon$-identifiability holds iff there is no trap of diameter $> \varepsilon$ (Theorem F1).
- **[Conj]** The continuum analogue holds with closed traps and asymptotic identification (Q-F3).

**Terminology check.** Existing vocabulary that covers parts of this:
- *feasible parameter set* / *membership set* (set-membership identification) for $\Theta_t$;
- *identified set* (partial identification) for irreducible uncertainty;
- *self-confirming* beliefs (Fudenberg–Levine) and *incomplete learning* (Easley–Kiefer) for learning that stops because the chosen actions are uninformative;
- *safely reachable set* (Sui et al.) for the closure of certified-safe expansion.

We keep "trap" as a working name only. See `research/literature_review.md` §3 before adopting any name.

## 5. The scalar model (analysed in `research/scalar_analysis.md`)

- $\theta^\* > 0$, $\Theta_0 = [L_0, U_0]$.
- $u = x \ge 0$, safe iff $\theta^\* x \le 1$.
- $Y = \theta^\* x^p + \eta$, with $|\eta| \le \delta$ and $p > 0$.
- Normalisation: $v = U/\theta^\*$, $\lambda = 2\delta(\theta^\*)^{p-1}$.

## 6. Later models (not analysed in Stage 0)

- **E1 Hard constraint (the present model).** $g(\theta^\*, u_t) \le 0$ for every $t$, every $\theta^\* \in \Theta_0$ and every $\eta$.
- **E2 Almost-sure safety under stochastic noise.** Bounded-support noise gives E1 again; unbounded noise generally forces E1 with $\Theta_t$ never shrinking to a set, so the model must be specified.
- **E3 Finite-horizon chance constraint.** $\sup_{\theta}\Pr^\pi_\theta(\exists t \le T : g(\theta, u_t) > 0) \le \epsilon$.
- **E4 Lifetime budget.** As E3 with $T = \infty$.
- **E5 Bayesian (prior over $\theta$).** $\Pr$ includes the prior; this is a different object from E3.

E3 and E4 need randomised or noisy-observation policies for the risk to be anything other than 0 or 1 at a fixed $\theta$. This is easy to get wrong and must be fixed before any Direction E statement.

## 7. Notation conventions

- Intervals are closed.
- "Safe" includes equality, $g = 0$.
- Capture-style boundary effects (open versus closed sets) are tracked explicitly whenever a result is sharp.
